How it works

Why three, and why you can trust them.

Start searching
1
Tell us who and when

Three chips: who you are with, what the occasion is, and one hard constraint. Ten seconds, not a form.

2
Get three, with reasons

Not forty. Three, each carrying the one sentence that explains why it beat the rest, plus how close the next place was.

Each card carries the reason, the rank, the gap to the next place, and the exact number of people behind it. If a place only has 61 ratings, we say so instead of rounding it into confidence.

3
Log what you go to

One comparison against a place you already ranked — was it better than this one? Two taps. Nobody has to invent what four-and-a-half stars means, and the more you log, the more your comparisons count.

Same mechanic the app uses "Was Chutneys better than Simply South?" — a binary call, inserted straight into your ranked list at the right position.

About Madli

Madli started because deciding where to eat had become a research project. Forty tabs, three review sites, and a nagging sense that the top result had paid to be there.

We rank places by asking the people who actually go there one simple question at a time — was this better than that? Comparisons are harder to game than stars, and they mean something specific. Someone who has logged forty biryani places in Old City knows more about biryani in Old City than someone passing through, so their comparisons carry more weight. That is the entire model.

We do not sell rank. A business can claim its listing and correct its own phone number and timings. It cannot move itself up.
